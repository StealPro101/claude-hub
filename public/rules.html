Rules

Short, and meant plainly.

Putting a game on the Hub

  • Only games in a public repo on your own GitHub account. The Hub plays it from your own address, never from a link you type.
  • Your repo is public, because that is how the Hub finds it. On the Hub the game stays private until you publish it yourself.
  • No forks and no copies of someone else’s work. Your own game, or something you are allowed to use — and keep its licence notice.
  • No keys, tokens, passwords or personal information in a game, ever.
  • Nothing hateful, nothing sexual, and nothing made to harm, scare or trick whoever plays it.
  • Never ask players for a login, an account or money.
  • Up to twenty games each.

The boring part

  • The Hub owner can remove or hide any game or member, at any time.
  • GitHub’s own terms apply to your account and anything you put on it.
  • Signing in tells the Hub who you are — name, picture, bio — and nothing else. No scopes are asked for and your GitHub token is never kept.
  • A cover picture is re-drawn in your browser before it is sent, so the location and camera data a phone writes into a photo never reach the Hub.
  • Games run on their maker’s own page, in a sandbox. The Hub does not check anyone’s code — play at your own risk.

Something wrong with a game?

Tell the Hub owner: which game, and what is wrong. He can hide it in seconds. If it is dangerous or against GitHub’s terms, report the repo on GitHub too.
