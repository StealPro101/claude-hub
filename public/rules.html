Sharing a game

  • Only games in a public repo on your own GitHub account. The address is worked out from your account, never from a link you type.
  • Your repo is public because that is how it is found. Here the game stays private until you publish it yourself.
  • No forks and no copies of someone else’s work. Keep any licence notice.
  • No keys, tokens, passwords or personal information in a game, ever.
  • Nothing hateful, nothing sexual, and nothing made to harm, scare or trick whoever plays it.
  • Never ask players for a login, an account or money.
  • Up to twenty games each.

The boring part

  • The owner can hide or remove any game or member, at any time.
  • GitHub’s own terms apply to your account and anything on it.
  • Signing in says who you are — name, picture, bio — and nothing else. No scopes are asked for and your GitHub token is never kept.
  • A cover picture is re-drawn in your browser before it is sent, so the location a phone writes into a photo never arrives here.
  • You can switch “show when I’m online” off on your games page.
  • Games run on their makers’ own pages, in a sandbox. Nobody here checks their code — play at your own risk.

Something wrong with a game?

Open the game and press Report. That sends the owner one line with the game and your name, and nothing else. If it is dangerous or against GitHub’s terms, report the repo on GitHub as well.
